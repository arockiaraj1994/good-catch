## What happened

On Saturday 17 October 2026, the No Kings Coalition held a national day of protest in the US. During it, Instagram took the group's account offline. The group was also using the page to encourage people to vote early before the 3 November midterm elections.

The page was down for about 90 minutes, and the group's organisers were locked out for longer. An automatic review found about an hour later that the account should not have been removed.

Andy Stone, a Meta spokesperson, called it an error and wrote: "We want to reiterate our sincere apology for the error."

## Why it went wrong

Meta says a tool linked to Anthropic's Claude was reading the account's profiles, posts and hashtags about four times faster than normal, in short bursts. To Meta's automatic protections, that looked like a bot, so they shut the account down.

Meta has not said which tool it was, who was running it or what it was doing. Nothing Meta has said suggests the tool broke any rule.

## Impact

A political group lost its main way to reach supporters on its busiest day, in the middle of its push to get people to vote early. Organisers criticised Meta, saying Instagram is a key way to reach their supporters.

## The lesson

Automatic systems that shut accounts down need a fast way for a person to check and reverse them, especially at times that matter, like protests and elections.
