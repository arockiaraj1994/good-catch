## What happened

The US Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) ended work-from-home for many staff in 2025. Their union, the National Treasury Employees Union, took the case to an arbitrator, who ruled for the workers in January 2026. HHS appealed to the Federal Labor Relations Authority, the board that hears these disputes.

On 21 October 2026 the board turned down the appeal. It also looked closely at HHS's written arguments. It found that 22 of the brief's 29 footnotes, and 35 of its 42 citations, were "fictitious, erroneous, or irrelevant."

The board wrote: "it appears that the Agency may have used a generative artificial-intelligence (AI) tool" to draft the brief.

## Why it went wrong

Writing tools can produce citations that look real but point to cases that never existed, or to cases that say something else. If nobody checks each one against the real record, the mistakes go straight into the filing.

HHS has not said whether it used an AI tool.

## Impact

HHS lost the appeal. The board said nothing stops people using AI tools in filings, but warned that "AI continues to present significant risks of error" and that parties should check anything such a tool produces. It did not punish the department.

HHS has until 21 December 2026 to take the case to a federal appeals court.

## The lesson

A citation is a promise that a source exists and says what you claim. Look up every one yourself, especially in legal papers.
