## What happened

In February 2023, Google showed off Bard, its answer to ChatGPT. The launch ad asked the chatbot a simple question: "What new discoveries from the James Webb Space Telescope can I tell my 9-year-old about?"

Bard gave three answers. One said the James Webb telescope "took the very first pictures of a planet outside of our own solar system."

That is wrong. The first photo of such a planet (an exoplanet) was taken in 2004, by a telescope in Chile run by the European Southern Observatory. The James Webb telescope did not even launch until 2021. Astronomers spotted the error within hours.

## Why it went wrong

- **A classic AI hallucination.** Chatbots like Bard run on large language models (LLMs). They are built to write answers that *sound* right, not to check facts. When they don't know, they can make something up and say it with full confidence.
- **"First" claims are a trap.** Records, firsts and "biggest ever" facts are exactly where generative AI slips most often.
- **Nobody fact-checked it.** The answer went into a global launch ad without anyone comparing it to a trusted source.

## Impact

- The mistake was in Google's own launch ad, so it was reported around the world.
- Shares in Google's parent company, Alphabet, fell sharply the next day. News reports widely linked the drop to the error.
- It became the go-to example of an AI hallucination.

## The lesson

1. **Fact-check before you share.** Be extra careful with dates, records and "firsts".
2. **Confident is not the same as correct.** An AI chatbot sounds just as sure when it is wrong.
3. **Big moments need extra checks.** If this slipped past Google, it can slip past anyone.
