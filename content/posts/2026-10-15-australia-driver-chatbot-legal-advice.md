## What happened

A delivery driver in Australia took Sterling Parts to court, saying he had been unfairly let go. He told the court that 38 documents proved his case.

Despite being asked many times, he never handed any of them over. In August 2026, Judge Catherine Symons of the Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia threw the case out.

She called it "a cautionary tale about the dangers of befriending artificial intelligence (AI)-powered chatbots who masquerade as legal advisors."

## Why it went wrong

The documents he listed were never produced, and the judge's warning makes clear the court saw chatbot "legal advice" behind the case. The judge's point was simple: "AI is not a lawyer, does not hold a practising certificate and is not authorised in any jurisdiction in Australia to engage in legal practice."

## Impact

- His claim was never heard on its merits. It ended because the evidence never appeared.
- Time and money lost for him, the company and the court.

## The lesson

1. **Only list evidence you actually have.** If you can't hand it over, it doesn't count.
2. **Get real legal help if you can.** Many places have free legal advice for workers.
3. **A chatbot can sound like a lawyer without being one.** It won't be in court with you.
