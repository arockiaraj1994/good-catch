## What happened

A Romanian lawyer was helping a client bring a case to the European Court of Human Rights. She used AI tools to help write her written arguments to the court.

The papers had many errors about the court's own past decisions. They pointed to cases that do not exist. They quoted paragraphs that are not in real judgments. And they relied on real cases that had nothing to do with the point, or that said the opposite.

On 19 November 2026 the court said its president had approved a two-year ban: she cannot represent anyone before the court in that time. The other lawyer on the same case is not affected.

## Why it went wrong

AI writing tools can produce text that looks like a proper legal reference even when the case or the paragraph was never real. The lawyer admitted she used AI and had not checked all the cases she cited. She called the problems "inaccuracies" and citation errors.

The court called that an "understatement". It found "a pattern of numerous, repeated, substantive errors, rather than isolated citation mistakes", and said many of them would have shown up with a quick search of its own database of past decisions.

## Impact

- The court had to spend a lot of time checking her papers and writing to others involved, time it said should have gone to its real work of protecting human rights.
- Her client lost one of their two lawyers in the middle of the case.
- The court said she may be allowed to represent people again later.

## The lesson

1. **Check every case yourself.** The court said lawyers must check everything an AI tool gives them against trusted sources before filing it.
2. **Saying "I used AI" is not an excuse.** The lawyer is still responsible for every word she sends to a court.
3. **Mistakes cost other people time.** Made-up references slow down a court that many people are waiting on.
