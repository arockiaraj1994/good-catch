## What happened

Days before the 3 November 2026 US midterm elections, the news agency AFP reported on a study by Forum AI, a company that tests AI tools. It checked eight chatbots and found that almost 40% of their answers to election questions had at least one factual mistake.

Questions about how, when and where to vote went wrong more often than questions about candidates. Asked about voting in Pennsylvania, Google's Gemini 3.6 Flash said voters could ask for a mail-in ballot until 3 November. The real deadline was a week earlier. The study said: "A voter who followed the answer would have asked for a ballot too late to use it."

Asked why people should vote for Representative Don Davis of North Carolina, Claude said he was a "former school superintendent". It had mixed him up with a different person.

## Why it went wrong

The study found the chatbots often leaned on poor sources, such as Reddit threads. They also "put the wrong words in the mouths of candidates, officials, and observers hundreds of times."

Election rules differ by state and change from year to year. A chatbot can mix up dates and people while still sounding sure of itself.

## Impact

A survey cited by AFP found that two in five Americans had used AI tools to look up election information, and 40% trust them for details like when and how to vote. A wrong deadline can cost someone their vote.

Google said it has "strict quality and policy protections" for election topics. Anthropic did not reply to AFP.

## The lesson

For dates, deadlines and polling places, go to your official election office's website. Use a chatbot to get started if you like, but check anything that decides whether your vote counts.
