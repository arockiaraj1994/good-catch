## What happened

On 20 October 2025, students at Kenwood High School near Baltimore, in the US state of Maryland, were waiting to be picked up after football practice. An AI camera system made by Omnilert, which watches for weapons, flagged student Taki Allen. He was holding a bag of chips.

Police arrived with guns pointed at the boys. Camera footage from the officers shows them handcuffing and searching the students. There was no gun.

In November 2026, four of the students sued the school system, the county police and Omnilert.

## Why it went wrong

The system mistook an everyday snack for a weapon. According to Omnilert, the school's safety staff looked at the alert and cancelled it within about two minutes.

The lawsuit says the principal overrode that cancellation and sent the police anyway. It also says the officers had the picture of Allen with the chips but did not look at it before drawing their guns. The students' lawyer said there are "12 false positives a day" in Baltimore County alone.

## Impact

- The lawyer says the students now have "flashbacks, nightmares, anxiety".
- The lawsuit asks for better training and communication between the police and schools, safety limits on how the system is used, and for its use to be paused until then.
- Omnilert said what the students went through "should not have happened" and that "a canceled alert must stay canceled". The school system and police said they had no comment.

## The lesson

1. **An alarm is a guess, not a fact.** A person should check the picture before anyone points a gun at a child.
2. **When people check and say "false alarm", that must stick.** A cancelled alert should not turn back into an armed response.
3. **Frequent false alarms are dangerous.** Every wrong alert risks sending armed officers to children.
