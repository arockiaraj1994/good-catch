## What happened

Waymo runs taxis in the US that have no human driver. Since 1 October 2026, its cars have driven into freeway lanes that were closed for construction 11 times. On 9 November alone, three of them drove into lanes marked with cones as closed.

Waymo told the US road safety regulator that it is recalling nearly 4,500 vehicles to fix this with a software update. California's vehicle regulator had already banned Waymo cars from the state's freeways, and Waymo stopped freeway driving across the US.

A second recall is about how the cars respond to police officers directing traffic by hand at junctions. It follows a crash in which a Waymo car followed an officer's directions and the officer was then briefly blocked.

## Why it went wrong

The self-driving system did not reliably understand cones and lane closures for road works. Waymo describes "unusual circumstances where a Waymo vehicle may enter and continue driving in a closed freeway lane".

Waymo had already narrowed where its cars could drive to keep them away from closed freeway lanes. The three cars on 9 November still went in, just three days after that change.

## Impact

- Waymo says no one was hurt and there were no crashes in the closed-lane cases. It is still working on the fix.
- Road workers in closed lanes expect no traffic, so a car driving through is a real danger to them.
- Regulators are also looking into Waymo cars passing stopped school buses with flashing red lights. Most of those cases happened after Waymo said it had fixed the problem.

## The lesson

1. **Road works are where self-driving cars struggle.** Cones, closed lanes and people directing traffic change every day.
2. **Check that a fix really works.** Both here and with the school buses, problems continued after changes were made.
3. **Regulators matter.** The freeway ban came before Waymo's own recall.
