## What happened

In late July 2026, at the AIDS 2026 conference in Rio de Janeiro, US officials gave a talk about new global health plans. On screen was a map of Africa.

Every country on it was labelled wrong. Nigeria, which sits on the Atlantic coast, was shown as a landlocked country in the Sahara Desert. Mozambique had moved from the south-east of Africa to the Horn of Africa. Ivory Coast was on the wrong side of the continent.

The map had a mark showing it was made with an OpenAI tool.

## Why it went wrong

Picture-making AI tools are good at making things *look* like a map. They are not good at getting the facts on a map right. The State Department said the map "had been added after a last-minute change to the presentation by a member of the team," so nobody checked it.

## Impact

- At a conference about Africa's health, in front of African delegates, the US got Africa's map wrong.
- The State Department apologised and said it regretted the mistake, "particularly to African delegates attending the conference."

## The lesson

1. **Use real maps for real places.** Proper, checked maps are free and easy to find.
2. **Last-minute changes need a second look.** That's exactly when mistakes slip through.
3. **Small errors can say something bigger.** To the audience, this looked like not caring about getting Africa right.
