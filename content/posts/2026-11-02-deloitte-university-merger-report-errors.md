## What happened

The Western Australian government paid Deloitte A$166,000 for a 219-page study on merging three universities: the University of Western Australia, Curtin and Murdoch.

In October 2026, readers found dozens of mistakes. Some references pointed to sources that do not exist. Some sums were wrong: in one place two costs added up to A$659 million, but the report gave A$399 million. The report also said one option had the "largest benefit-cost ratio", while its own summary showed otherwise.

Deloitte's report says it used AI tools to gather research, summarise and proofread. Deloitte called the mistakes "minor referencing errors".

## Why it went wrong

AI writing tools can invent references that look real. They can also slip in numbers that do not match. If nobody checks every source and sum by hand, the mistakes stay in the final report.

## Impact

Education minister Tony Buti said the government had told Deloitte of its "anger and concern". Deloitte must fix the report and issue it again. The state is asking for its money back quickly.

The government says the mistakes do not change the report's findings. Murdoch's vice-chancellor called the cost-benefit analysis "nonsense".

In 2025 Deloitte already partly refunded Australia's federal government over a different report with similar errors.

## The lesson

If a report helps decide something big, check its sources and sums before you rely on it. Paying a well-known firm does not mean the checking was done.
